0. How to read this

Three things are kept strictly separate throughout, because merging them is how a knowledge base rots:

Mark Meaning
D Dictated. Stated by the principal. Authoritative on intent and practice.
V Verified. Confirmed in a primary TNSDC governance document, with date/reference.
C Conflict. Dictation and documents disagree. Flagged, not resolved. Needs a ruling.
G Gap. Present in documents or logically required, but absent from the dictation. Needs confirmation.
I Inference. My structural reading. Not a fact. Reject freely.

Every step carries a stable ID (S3.4). Do not renumber them. They are the join key for the SOP, the Gantt, the RACI, the tracking ledger schema, and any automation built later. New steps get decimal insertions (S3.4a), never a renumber.

S3 — Preliminary Mapping & Commercials

This is the analytically hardest stage and the one most worth automating. It is a constrained allocation problem, not a spreadsheet exercise (see §9.3).

S3.1 — Establish the demand baseline (must precede mapping)

D Before any mapping, establish:

  • Student count per district
  • Student count per domain within each district
  • Student count per zone

V Zones in use: N1, N2, W1, W2, C1, C2, S1, S2, SE, CH (10). District-level allocation also occurs (e.g. Tiruppur, Erode, Namakkal, Salem, Dharmapuri, Krishnagiri, Coimbatore, Nilgiris named individually).

S3.2 — Project forward

D Projected students = 1.25 × previous semester's count for the same unit. I Confirm the unit of application — per district, per domain, or per district × domain. OQ-9.

S3.3 — Course-to-district assignment (coverage layer)

Constraints, all D:

# Constraint Type
C1 Pull the previous two semesters' mappings — which course went to which district Input
C2 No district may receive the same course in consecutive semesters — the same student cohort moves up, so courses must be swapped Hard
C3 Every domain in every district must have a course assigned. No domain in any district may be left without one Hard
C4 Only courses for which the CEC cleared at least one TP are eligible Hard
C5 Sufficient course variety per district; sufficient TPs statewide Soft/goal

S3.4 — TP-to-district assignment (allocation layer)

Constraints, all D unless marked:

# Constraint Type
A1 Districts this TP has handled before → prefer Soft, positive
A2 New TP? → must not receive a large student allocation Soft, strong
A3 Number of students handled before Input
A4 Past performance/review from feedback forms Input
A5 Honour stated preferred districts and zones Soft
A6 No course may be monopolised by a single TP — unless that TP is a long-term partner. D "this rule is based on the given circumstance, and might vary wildly based on the discretion of the people involved" Soft, discretionary
A7 33% revenue cap: students_allocated × cost_per_student ≤ 0.33 × (3-year average revenue). Revenue figure sourced from DV (E1) Hard, quantitative
A8 V Allocation proportional to the number of TRET-qualified trainers Hard — but this can only bind at S4.6, after TRET results exist

Quantified consequence of A7 I: at the ₹1 crore eligibility floor, a TP's ceiling is ₹33,00,000 ÷ cost_per_student.

  • At ₹600/student → 5,500 students maximum.
  • At ₹1,450/student → 2,275 students maximum. V Observed 2026-27 Odd allocations: L&T EduTech 2,895 students @ ₹1,450 = ₹41,97,750 → implies a 3-year average revenue of at least ₹1.27 crore for that TP alone. The cap is a live, binding constraint at realistic allocation sizes, not a formality. It must be computed before the A3 sheet is printed, not after.

# Step Detail
S3.6.1 Fix the date, convene the committee D Financial members + board members + deciding authorities. V Held 15.05.2026 at TNSDC office; official verb is "moderation".
S3.6.2 Deliberate course → TP → price D Which course to which TP, and at what rate.

D Mail to each TP requesting confirmation of course + price + districts. If confirming, the TP must return:

S4 — Trainer Qualification (TRET) & Remapping

# Step Detail
S4.1 Plan the TRET D Exams for all trainers under each TP, for the subjects they will teach.
S4.2 Commission question papers D Drafted by a separate external contractor — structurally independent of delivery.
S4.3 Internal vetting D Vetted by the internal team responsible for course planning, pedagogy and assessment. Their confirmation is a hard gate.
S4.4 Hand off to assessment partners D Paper + options released to assessment partners, who build and conduct the online test with TNSDC.
S4.5 Arrange venues D Negotiate with colleges in each district for time slots and computer systems.
S4.6 Conduct and collect results D
S4.7 Sufficiency test D For each TP × course × district: are there enough qualified trainers plus substitutes to cover every district the TP committed to?

S4.8 — Shortfall handling (the rework branch)

D Cascade, in order:

  1. TP has insufficient qualified trainers for a committed district → TP may be dropped for that course/district.
  2. If dropping that TP leaves a course with only one TP, and that TP is not a long-term partner → constraint A6 is violated → the course itself must be dropped.
  3. Dropping a course breaks constraint C3 (every domain in every district must have a course) → full remap required.
  4. Second mapping pass executes with real trainer supply known: which course, which domain/department, which district, which TP, how many students.
  5. Internal decision: conduct a second TRET, or proceed with the current count. D OQ-12: what is the decision rule here? Currently undocumented and discretionary.
  6. Confirmation round #2 — mail to TPs stating course, student count, trainer count, districts; TP accepts the count and terms.

I This is the single highest-risk transition in the cycle. It sits after CEC, after CSC pricing, and after confirmation #1 — i.e. at the point of maximum sunk coordination cost — and its trigger (TRET pass rates) is not under TNSDC's control and is not forecast anywhere in the process. See §9.2.

Artifacts out: TRET question papers (contractor) · vetting sign-off · TRET results register · trainer sufficiency matrix (TP × course × district × qualified + substitutes) · final mapping (post-TRET) · confirmation mail #2 + TP responses.

6. The Master Tracking Ledger — the thing you own

D Stated requirement, verbatim in substance: a solid record of everyone who applied to the EoI, which subject they applied for, who came for and cleared document verification, and who came for and cleared each subsequent step — per subject. Clean, well tracked, because it will come into question for audit, RTI and transparency, and to be accountable for the government selection process.

I That is a description of a state machine over a well-defined entity, and getting the entity right is the whole design. Four entities, not one:

6.1 Entity model

APPLICATION            = (TP_applicant × Course)        ← the primary tracked entity
  |
  └─ carries: state, timestamps, evidence links, per-gate outcome

TP_PROFILE             = (TP_applicant)
  |
  └─ carries: 3-yr avg revenue, trainer count, years in service, trainees delivered,
              preferred zones/districts (capture #1 and #2), prior-cycle performance

ALLOCATION             = (Course × TP × Domain × District/Zone × College)
  |
  └─ carries: projected student count, cost per student, total cost, Skill Offering ID

TRAINER                = (TP × Trainer × Course)
  |
  └─ carries: contract status, TRET attempt, TRET result, districts committed,
              primary or substitute

Why APPLICATION and not TP: a TP can pass DV once but pass CEC for UI/UX and fail for GenAI, be mapped in three districts and dropped in one after TRET. Tracking at TP level loses all of that. The row is the pair.

6.2 State machine for APPLICATION

EOI_SUBMITTED
  → DV_INVITED → DV_NO_SHOW ⊗
               → DV_ATTENDED → DV_FAILED ⊗
                             → DV_CLEARED
  → CCAR_PENDING → CCAR_OVERDUE ⊗        (2-week cure, due 1 week before CEC)
                 → CCAR_RECEIVED
  → CEC_INVITED → CEC_NO_SHOW ⊗
                → CEC_ATTENDED → CEC_FAILED ⊗
                               → CEC_CLEARED
  → PRELIM_MAPPED  |  CLEARED_UNMAPPED        (cleared but no district assigned)
  → CSC_PRICED
  → CONFIRM1_SENT → CONFIRM1_DECLINED ⊗
                  → CONFIRM1_ACCEPTED
  → TRET_SCHEDULED → TRET_SHORTFALL → DROPPED ⊗
                                    → REMAPPED  ⟲ back to PRELIM_MAPPED
                   → TRET_SUFFICIENT
  → FINAL_MAPPED
  → CONFIRM2_ACCEPTED
  → SUBCOMMITTEE_APPROVED
  → CAC_APPROVED
  → BOARD_APPROVED
  → WORK_ORDER_ISSUED   ← status flips to TRAINING PARTNER
  → DELIVERING → COMPLETED
⊗ = terminal exit. Every ⊗ needs a recorded reason code.

I The audit defence is the exit reasons, not the pass list. An RTI query will not ask who succeeded — it will ask why a particular applicant did not. Every ⊗ transition must carry a reason code and a link to the evidencing artifact (DV checklist row, CEC template row, TRET result row). Without that, the ledger records what happened and cannot explain why, which is exactly the question that gets asked.

9. Structural findings

9.1 The critical path is not the delivery — it is S3→S4→S5

Everything from S6 onward is execution against a fixed plan. Everything from S0 to S5 is a serial approval chain with no parallelism, where each body can only sit after the previous one has produced its paper. The observed 2026-27 Odd calendar makes the shape visible:

06.02 EoI ad → 23.02/06.04 windows close → 27-28.02/08.05 CEC → 15.05 CSC → 16.06 Sub-Cttee → 23.06 CAC → 04.07 Board note → work orders → FDP → delivery.

That is five months from advertisement to board note, for a 60-hour course. The schedule risk is concentrated entirely before a single student is taught. Any Gantt built from this model should show S0–S5 as the critical path and S6–S8 as the buffer-free tail.

9.2 The TRET rework loop is the largest unpriced risk

The loop S4.8 → S3.3 is the only backward edge in the whole process that can fire after pricing, after confirmation #1, and potentially after committee papers have been drafted. Its cascade is three-deep and fully specified in your own account:

insufficient trainers → TP dropped → course left with one non-long-term TP → course dropped → a domain-district loses coverage → full remap.

Three observations:

  1. The trigger is exogenous. TRET pass rate is determined by TP hiring quality and question difficulty. Nothing upstream forecasts it. You discover the number at S4.6 and must react.
  2. Nothing upstream hedges it. The trainer count collected at S3.7 is declared, not qualified. The gap between declared and qualified is the entire risk, and it is never estimated in advance.
  3. The cheapest fix is a prior, not a process change. Last cycle's TRET pass rate per TP per course is already in your records. Applying it as a haircut at S3.4 — allocate against declared_trainers × historical_pass_rate rather than declared_trainers — converts a rework loop into an up-front discount. That is a two-column change to the mapping sheet.

Falsifiable prediction: if TRET pass rate is not modelled at S3.4, at least one course will require remapping in any cycle where a new TP holds a majority allocation for that course. Test it against the 2026-27 Odd record — if no remap occurred, the pass rates were high enough to mask the exposure, not absent.

9.3 S3 is a constraint satisfaction problem wearing a spreadsheet costume

Stated plainly, S3.3 + S3.4 is: assign (course, TP) pairs to (district, domain) cells subject to three hard coverage constraints (C2, C3, C4), one hard financial constraint (A7), one soft anti-concentration constraint (A6), and four soft preference constraints (A1, A2, A4, A5) — with the objective of full coverage.

That is a textbook constrained assignment problem. Consequences worth stating:

  • It may be infeasible, and infeasibility is informative. If C3 (every domain covered in every district) cannot be satisfied given the CEC-cleared TP pool and the A7 revenue caps, that is not a mapping failure — it is evidence that the EoI under-sourced a domain, and it should trigger S1.6a (re-float), which is exactly what happened with PCB Design & Testing in Phase 2. Currently that diagnosis is reached by hand, late. A feasibility check run immediately after S2.10 would surface it weeks earlier.
  • Automation target, ranked. If you automate one thing in this process, automate the A7 headroom calculation — it is pure arithmetic on data you already collect at DV, it is a hard constraint, and violating it is the kind of error that surfaces in audit. Automating the full assignment is second; it requires encoding A6's discretion, which is explicitly political and should stay human.
  • The discretion in A6 is a feature, not sloppiness — but it must be logged. A discretionary monopoly exception with no recorded rationale is the single most attackable decision in the whole cycle under RTI.

9.4 Two structural holes

  • No TP payment step (OQ-5). The process ends at certificates. The board note conditions payment on learning outcomes. Between "marks uploaded" and "money disbursed" there is an unmodelled verification-and-invoice process that someone owns.
  • No outcome tracking beyond the certificate. Feedback is collected on how the course went, not on what happened to the student. Every downstream allocation decision (A4, "past performance from feedback forms") therefore rests on satisfaction data, not employment data. That is a known structural weakness of the wider Indian skilling apparatus and it is worth naming, though fixing it is well outside this process model's scope.

9.5 Strongest objection to this model

It over-formalises a process whose real flexibility is load-bearing. A6's discretion, the "corresponding authorities" of S3.5, and the second-TRET decision at S4.8.5 are deliberately unspecified because the people involved need room to move. A rigid SOP that closes those gaps could make the process less operable while looking more rigorous on paper.

The defence: the model marks those three points as explicitly discretionary rather than specifying them away. The parts being hardened — the ledger, the DV checklist as a value-capture instrument, the A7 arithmetic, the reason codes on exits — are the parts that carry audit exposure and where discretion buys nothing.
